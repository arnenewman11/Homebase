Goal and how I'll measure it

  • Increase coaching hours and income as fast as possible. Nothing is off the table.
  • Raise my blended hourly rate by leaning on higher-paying formats (not mentioned to my boss).
  • Success: hours on the calendar, attendance sustained for at least 1 month, private lessons generated from classes, and total coaching revenue.
  • Run a class with 1+ person; 2–4 is solid; 5+ earns the per-person bonus.

Pay math that shapes format choices

$25/hr

Classes and clinics, plus $5 per person over 4. A class needs about 11 players to match a 4-person game session.

60%

3-and-me and 4-player sessions, and lessons. Lesson club rates are $65 / $80 / $90 / $100 per hour for 1–4 people.

$60/hr

My share of a 4-person lesson. Small-group formats carry the blended rate; classes fill hours and feed lessons.

Decisions so far

Test, don't perfect

Try a few formats and times, keep what works. The first proposal doesn't need to be optimal.

Window: 9 am–5 pm, Mon–Fri

Start no earlier than 9 am; 8 am only if needed. No weekday time protected for other work.

Don't compete with what's working

Avoid displacing or duplicating well-booked offerings. Other coaches only matter if they target the same audience.

Structured formats in 2–5 pm

Afternoon open play now runs daily, so add clinics and small groups that complement it rather than more open play.

Sample 2–5 pm too

Early survey results point to appetite for afternoons, so the list covers late morning, lunch, and afternoon.

Survey: not relied on yet

Few responses so far, and respondents skew toward people who already attend evenings. Used only as a hint.

Short email to my boss

Proposed times and class types, with one short sentence of justification at most. She prefers minimal explanation.

Patterns across clubs

  • Coached, small-capacity, high-energy sessions fill at midday. Longmont runs two or three Cardio Pickle sessions between 11 and 2 and most fill at $24–32 per person. Midday open play is weak almost everywhere, including at Wheat Ridge.
  • Series beat drop-ins. Month-long and 4–5 week series fill (Gates monthly drills, Longmont Road to Gold, Mile Hi ladies' series). One-off topic clinics and drop-in drills mostly sit empty.
  • Affinity groups draw daytime players. Women-only and 50+/60+ sessions are among the best-filled daytime offerings at every club.
  • Early afternoon (1–3) works for seniors and mid-level open play; 2–5 all-levels open play doesn't. After 3:30, Gates and River Point shift to youth programs.
  • Price matters less than format. $10 open play sits empty while $24–32 coached sessions fill. The one premium test ($75 Play With a Pro) has no signups yet.
  • Daytime beginner programming is weak everywhere it's tried (Longmont 102 at 2 pm, 0/4). Gates' 2.0 monthly instruction at 9 am (7/12) is the exception.

What the market scan suggests for this list

  • Run tests as 4-week series where possible, not one-off drop-ins.
  • Favor a coached, high-energy small-group format (drills plus games, cardio-style) for the 11–1 slots.
  • Consider a women-only or 50+ version of one session. New idea: Tue or Thu 1–3 pm coached session for 50+ or mid-level players.
  • Treat Monday 11–12 daytime New to Pickleball as a lower-confidence test; beginner daytime programs struggle elsewhere.
  • Price benchmarks: coached small-group sessions $24–32 per person per hour; 4-week series about $100–170; monthly drill sessions $55–89.
