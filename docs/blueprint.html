1 · The big idea

Switch from a web of syncs to a hub with spokes

Right now several places each think they own a task's status, and syncing keeps them in step. Every extra connection is another place for a check-off to get lost. A hub puts one store in the middle; everything else either feeds it or reads from it.

i
Why structure comes before the orchestrator

An AI helper working on a messy system has to rediscover where everything lives each time it runs, which is slow, expensive and inconsistent. Give it a fixed format and a written map first, and the same helper becomes a short, cheap, predictable job.

2 · Key terms

Seven words this blueprint leans on

Each comes with an everyday comparison. You won't need more jargon than this.

Canonical storeLike: the one master calendar on the fridge

The single place whose answer wins. Homebase's built-in database plays this role.

SchemaLike: a form with fixed boxes to fill in

The list of fields every task must have. It's what lets tasks from different places line up and be sorted the same way.

ManifestLike: the label map on a breaker panel

A written inventory of every part of the system and what it's allowed to touch. Helpers read it instead of searching.

Scheduled taskLike: a recurring calendar reminder that does the work itself

A Claude run that starts on a timer, such as the daily sort or the Monday review, without you being there.

ConnectorLike: a key to one room of your house

Claude's link to an app such as Gmail or Drive. Each key can be limited to reading only.

SkillLike: a recipe card Claude follows

Saved instructions for doing one job the same way every time, such as turning roadmap items into tasks.

ArtifactLike: a private web page Claude made for you

Homebase, the Dakboard and the roadmaps are all artifacts. Private by default, one share away from anyone.

3 · Architecture

How the pieces fit together

Everything enters through one inbox, only the Homebase database holds status, and every screen reads from it.

i
Read it left to right

Ideas come in on the left. The inbox holds them raw. Each morning the sort files them into the store with all their fields filled. The screens on the right only read; checking a box on any of them updates the store, not a separate copy. The steward and the export are the only other things that touch the store, and both run on a schedule.

Every task fills in the same form

id never changes, even if the title does. That's what keeps a check-off on the Dakboard pointed at the right task.
next_step is required once a task is active. It's the "where I left off" line that makes picking things back up easy.
milestone ties the task to a roadmap phase. It drives progress bars and the bigger points awards.
raw keeps your dictated words exactly as spoken, so nothing is lost if the sort misreads you.
changed_by records whether you, the sort, the steward or the intake skill made the last edit. That's your audit trail.

One firm rule

No money amounts, balances or account details ever go in a task. A finance task says what to do ("Review October spending categories"), never the numbers.

6 · Capture and routing

Capture fast now, sort smart later

Capturing should take seconds and no thinking. Sorting happens once a day, before your wall switches to morning mode, using each area's roadmap for context.

Worked example: one idea, start to finish

You dictateOn your phone, mic button in Homebase's quick-add:
"get the topps lot listed this weekend"
It waits in the inboxSaved instantly with status inbox and your raw words.
The morning sort reads itMatches it to Card Sales, fills bucket, type, effort, and a next step from that roadmap.
It shows up readyOn Homebase and the wall as next. If the sort wasn't sure, it asks you with one tap.

i
Why not one big chat thread?

A single thread grows long, and every new message re-sends its whole history, so it gets slower and more expensive. It also mixes every project's context together. One inbox with many destinations keeps capture light and lets context come from the right area.

7 · The steward

A weekly check-up, not a robot running the house

Every Monday morning, before your priorities review, the steward looks over the whole system and reports what it found. Findings appear in a Homebase panel and in your review.

✓ Does on its own

  • Fixes small field drift (missing area, stale flag)
  • Links obvious duplicates
  • Adds tasks a roadmap lists but the store lacks
  • Keeps the manifest's facts current

? Recommends to you

  • New or retired areas
  • Changes to the schema
  • New connectors or scheduled jobs
  • Model policy changes
  • Anything that deletes

✕ Never

  • Sends email or messages
  • Shares or publishes anything
  • Touches finance data
  • Overrides your edits

What it checks

CheckExample finding
Health"3 tasks have been in doing for over 14 days"
Drift"Arnfjäll roadmap lists 2 items the store doesn't have"
Balance"Learn & Play has had no activity for 3 weeks"
Operations"Tuesday's sort didn't run; Sunday's export completed"
Cost"All jobs ran on their assigned models"

Phone alerts are only for real problems: a job failing twice in a row, an unexpected permission change, or Homebase data being unreachable.

Think of the three models as staff with different rates. You wouldn't have a senior architect file paperwork, and you wouldn't have a clerk redesign the house.

The architectOpus

Habits that save the most

Point helpers at the manifest

Reading one map beats searching your whole account every run.

Start a new thread per topic

Long threads re-send their entire history with each message.

Keep scheduled prompts short and fixed

Each run pays for its instructions; fixed wording also gives steady results.

Batch small asks

Five requests in one message cost less than five separate messages.

i
How the policy improves

Once a month, the Monday review includes a short check: what ran on which model, anything that felt slow or weak, and one suggested adjustment for you to accept or skip. One honest limit: Claude can't see your account's usage dashboard, so costs are estimated, not measured.

9 · Security and durability

Three real risks, each with a specific guard

Risk · A link exposes personal data

Artifacts are private, but one share away from anyone. The Dakboard may read Homebase through a link.

Guard

Phase 1 audits the Dakboard connection. If it's a shared link, it's replaced with a view showing only titles and calendar. The client demo is a separate copy with sample data.

Risk · An unattended run does something permanent

Connectors can send, share and delete.

Guard

Scheduled runs only get the permissions in the connector table. Sending, sharing and deleting always wait for you.

Risk · A bad instruction hides in an email

Text from emails or web pages could try to tell a helper what to do.

Guard

Imported content can create a task, but it's always treated as data, never as an instruction.

Risk · Silent failure

A broken sort quietly lets things go stale.

Guard

Every write is logged, the steward checks run history weekly, and two failures in a row push to your phone.

Risk · Losing everything

Homebase's data lives inside one artifact.

Guard

Weekly JSON and CSV export to Drive, keeping 8 weeks. The blueprint is exported monthly too.

Risk · Secrets in the wrong place

Keys or logins pasted into tasks or pages.

Guard

Logins live only inside connectors; never in artifacts, tasks, memory or docs. No finance amounts in Homebase.

11 · Build plan

Four phases, each useful on its own

Phase 1 · Foundation start here

  • Audit Homebase's data and the Dakboard connection
  • Move Homebase to the task schema, keeping existing check-offs
  • Migrate open items from the 11 roadmaps, with source and milestone
  • Switch roadmaps to read-only status
  • Archive the older eBay roadmap and mark reference docs
  • Save the manifest to Drive with this blueprint

Phase 2 · Capture and backups

  • Add the inbox and quick-add box to Homebase
  • Build the intake skill for threads and roadmaps
  • Create the Gmail label and daily early-morning sort
  • Set up the weekly Drive export

Phase 3 · Steward and policy

  • Create the Monday steward review and its panel
  • Add failure alerts and a change log view
  • Apply the model policy to every scheduled job

Phase 4 · Incentives and demo

  • Replace points and levels with milestone-based points
  • Add your rewards list and spend flow
  • Generate the sanitized client demo
